Loft conversion junction details

Construction details for a loft conversion: the dormer cheek where it lands on the main roof, the dormer flat roof at the cheek head, the eaves where the sloping ceiling meets the dwarf wall, the new loft floor into the existing wall, and the existing gable where it becomes part of the thermal envelope. Every dimension is taken from a clause already verified in the specification library, so the drawings and the specification agree. Where a detail and a clause ever disagree, the clause is right and the drawing is wrong.

These are library details, not project details. Status is For Building Control Approval. Foundations, beams and lintels are indicative and to the structural engineer's design. Print to A3 landscape; each sheet is one page and measures true at 1:10.

D-217

Dormer cheek to the main roof

Vertical section square to the main roof slope · 1:10 @ A3 landscape

What this sheet is for

Where the dormer cheek lands on the main roof, three things meet: the cheek insulation, the roof insulation, and a doubled trimmer sitting between them. It is also the junction where the weathering has to be soakers rather than a single flashing.

Sequence

Trimmer doubled to the engineer's design. Sole plate over it. Insulation packed round the plate and the trimmer before the cheek is sheathed — it cannot be done afterwards. Cheek framed and fully filled, vapour control layer, sheathing, breather membrane, 25 cavity, cladding. Soakers built in course by course as the covering goes on.

Critical check

The sole plate and the trimmer are 190mm of solid timber sitting exactly where the cheek insulation and the roof insulation want to meet. Insulate round them and the line is continuous; leave it and the cheek is bridged along its whole length.

Soakers, not a raking flashing. And the cavity barrier at the perimeter of the cheek is Approved Document B, not a thermal component — draw it separately and label it separately.

D-222

Existing gable to the new roof

Vertical section square to the roof slope · 1:10 @ A3 landscape

What this sheet is for

The gable becomes part of the thermal envelope when the loft is converted, and it meets a roof built to a different standard: the gable is a renovated element at 0.30, the roof is a new element at 0.15. The junction between two different standards is where the bridge sits.

Sequence

Existing wall inspected and made good. Lining set out so it runs up between the gable and the last rafter, not stopped at the ceiling line. Roof insulation cut tight to it. Vapour control layer continuous round the corner.

Critical check

Stop the gable lining at the sloping ceiling and the top 222mm of the gable is uninsulated for the full length of the room. Carry it up between the gable and the last rafter until it meets the K107, and draw the two touching.

Internal insulation on existing masonry raises the risk of interstitial condensation. Inspect the wall first; where 0.18 cannot reasonably be reached, Approved Document L allows a lesser standard on feasibility grounds — state the achieved value and the reasoning rather than leaving it unaddressed.

D-219

Dormer flat roof at the cheek head

Vertical section between joists · 1:10 @ A3 landscape

What this sheet is for

The head of the dormer cheek, where the warm deck meets the cheek and the head plate sits between them. The waterproofing is straightforward; the insulation line is not.

Sequence

Head plate fixed, joists hung or bearing, deck laid to a finished fall not flatter than 1:40. Vapour control layer fully bonded to the deck. Insulation carried out over the plate and turned down its face to meet the cheek before the two layers are laid break bonded and fully adhered. Edge trim, then the waterproofing dressed over it.

Critical check

Cold deck construction is not to be used. The insulation goes above the deck, the vapour control layer below it, and both are continuous — a dormer roof built cold will fail on condensation whatever the U-value says.

The head plate is 50mm of solid timber directly between the roof insulation and the cheek insulation. Carry the insulation down its outer face; the falls, the trim and the upstands are all easier to get right than this and all get more attention.

D-221

New loft floor into the existing wall

Vertical section between joists · 1:10 @ A3 landscape

What this sheet is for

The new floor is a fire floor and a sound floor before it is a structural one. The beam and the joist size are the engineer's; REI 30, Requirement E2 and the sealing of the perimeter are what building control checks here.

Sequence

Padstone, beam, joists hung or bearing at wall plate level, spanning independently of the existing ceiling joists. Quilt between the joists. Two layers of plasterboard with the joints staggered, taped and filled. Deck glued and screwed. Perimeters and penetrations sealed.

Critical check

Two layers of 12.5 with staggered joints, not one layer of 25. The fire resistance comes from the layering and the joint treatment, and a single board does not give it however thick it is.

Where the existing ceiling stays below the new floor, its condition and fire resistance are confirmed on site — it is not assumed to contribute, and a lath and plaster ceiling in poor condition is a finding, not a detail.

D-220

Eaves at a room in roof — dwarf wall and eaves void

Vertical section between rafters · 1:10 @ A3 landscape

What this sheet is for

The eaves of a room in roof, where the sloping ceiling, the dwarf wall and the new floor all meet and the thermal envelope has to turn two corners in about a metre. It is the loft junction that gets marked up most often.

Sequence

Floor built through to the wall. Insulation laid across the eaves void floor before the dwarf wall goes up — it cannot be done afterwards. Sole plate, studs fully filled, head plate to the rafters. Vapour control layer taken round both corners. Access hatch formed, insulated and draught sealed.

Critical check

The insulation has to run in an unbroken line from the sloping ceiling, down the face of the dwarf wall, and across the floor of the eaves void behind it, lapped and sealed at the foot. Two corners, and the one behind the wall is the one that gets missed because nobody can see it once the wall is up.

The void itself stays outside the envelope and is cross ventilated. Insulating the dwarf wall and then leaving the void floor open makes the ceiling below it the coldest surface in the house.
