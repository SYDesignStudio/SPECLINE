Extension junction details

Eleven construction details for a house extension: the bottom of the wall, the roof edge, the join to the existing house, the window head, jamb and cill, the eaves and verge of a pitched roof, the intermediate floor, the foundation and a level access threshold. Every dimension is taken from a clause already verified in the specification library, so the drawings and the specification agree. Where a detail and a clause ever disagree, the clause is right and the drawing is wrong.

These are library details, not project details. Status is For Building Control Approval. Foundations, beams and lintels are indicative and to the structural engineer's design. Print to A3 landscape; each sheet is one page and measures true at 1:10.

D-201

External wall to ground floor and damp proof course

Vertical section · 1:10 @ A3 landscape

What this sheet is for

It substantiates the ground floor perimeter thermal bridge and the continuity of the damp proof course with the damp proof membrane — the two things a plan checker looks for at the bottom of a wall.

Sequence

Hardcore in layers not exceeding 150 to a finished 150 minimum, blinded 50. Slab not less than 100 in GEN1 or RC20. DPM not less than 1200 gauge over the slab, turned up at the perimeter. Floor insulation, then 65 screed. DPC not less than 150 above external ground in both leaves.

Critical check

The 25 edge insulation must run from the underside of the screed down past the floor insulation, and the cavity insulation must come down to the DPC. Those two together are the only thing breaking the path from inside to outside through the slab edge.

If they do not overlap on the drawing, the psi-value claimed in SAP is not the detail being built.

D-202

Warm deck flat roof at the eaves

Vertical section · 1:10 @ A3 landscape

What this sheet is for

It substantiates the wall head thermal bridge and shows the cavity barrier at the top of the cavity. Those are two different components doing two different jobs at the same place, and they are routinely conflated.

Sequence

Firrings cut to a design fall not flatter than 1:80. Deck screwed down, continuous vapour control layer on the warm side with 150 laps taped and sealed, insulation in two break-bonded layers fully adhered, waterproofing dressed over a drip projecting not less than 25 over the gutter.

Critical check

Draw the cavity barrier and the insulation as separate components and label both. The barrier stops fire and smoke in the cavity; it does nothing thermally.

The cavity insulation must be carried up to meet the roof insulation, or the roof insulation returned down over the wall head. Show which one, and show them touching.

D-203

Flat roof abutment to the existing dwelling

Vertical section · 1:10 @ A3 landscape

What this sheet is for

This is the most-queried detail on a rear extension and the one most often built wrong. It substantiates weather-tightness and insulation continuity where the new roof meets the existing house.

Three components, three jobs

The cavity tray keeps water out of the cavity. The cover flashing keeps water off the upstand. The insulation keeps the heat in. They sit at the same junction and they are not substitutes for one another.

Critical check

Show the stop end explicitly. A cavity tray without stop ends simply discharges into the cavity, and it is the single commonest defect at this junction.

A drawing that shows two of the three components will be built with two of the three.

D-204

Window head — insulated lintel and cavity tray

Vertical section · 1:10 @ A3 landscape

What this sheet is for

It substantiates the head thermal bridge and the two things a plan checker looks for over any opening: a cavity tray that catches what the cavity sheds, and insulation carried through the head so the cavity closer, not the lintel, is the thermal path.

Sequence

Lintel bedded on whole blocks or bricks with not less than 150 end bearing. Cavity tray laid over it with stop ends before the masonry goes on. Weep holes formed in the perpends of the first course above the tray. Closer fixed, insulation cut tight to it, then the frame.

Critical check

The cavity insulation, the closer and the frame have to form one unbroken line. A gap at any of the three, and the lintel becomes the thermal path across the whole opening.

The lintel is drawn indicatively. Its size comes from the manufacturer's tables for the span and the load, and the maker's full design is to be submitted before installation.

D-205

Window jamb — cavity closer and vertical damp proof course

Horizontal section · 1:10 @ A3 landscape

What this sheet is for

It substantiates the reveal thermal bridge and the moisture path. Where the cavity is closed, the two leaves are bridged, so both the insulation and the damp proof course have to be continuous around the return.

Sequence

Outer leaf returned into the reveal. Cavity closer fixed the full height of the jamb, insulation cut tight against it. Vertical damp proof course at the reveal. Extra ties at 225 vertical centres within 225 of the unbonded jamb. Frame fixed, lapping the closer.

Critical check

This is the one of the three most often got wrong on site, because the closer is fitted after the insulation and a 20mm gap behind it is invisible once the frame is in.

The jamb is unbonded masonry: without the extra ties at 225 centres the reveal has no restraint.

D-206

Window cill — sub-cill, damp proof course and closer

Vertical section · 1:10 @ A3 landscape

What this sheet is for

It substantiates the cill thermal bridge and shows water being thrown clear of the wall. The cill is also where the guarding and safety glazing rules bite, because it fixes how far the opening sits above the floor.

Sequence

Closer fixed and the insulation cut tight to it. Damp proof course laid across, lapped down over the outer leaf with stop ends at each jamb. Sub-cill bedded to fall with the throat clear. Frame set on the cill, sealed. Internal cill board last.

Critical check

Where the cill is less than 800 above finished floor level and the drop outside exceeds 600, guarding or an opening restrictor is required by Approved Document K.

Where the window is an escape window, the openable area is not less than 0.33 m² and not less than 450 by 450, with the bottom of the openable area not more than 1100 above the floor and not less than 800 unless guarded.

D-207

Eaves at a pitched roof

Vertical section between rafters · 1:10 @ A3 landscape

What this sheet is for

It substantiates the wall head thermal bridge where a pitched roof is insulated at rafter level, and separates the two components that meet there: the cavity barrier, which is fire stopping, and the insulation, which is not.

Sequence

Cavity insulation carried up to the underside of the wall plate. Cavity barrier over it, closing the head of the cavity. Wall plate bedded and strapped. K107 cut tight between the rafters, starting at the plate arris so it meets the cavity insulation. Vapour control layer, then the K118 lining, joints taped.

Critical check

The cavity insulation and the roof insulation must meet over the wall plate. Draw the K106 up to the underside of the plate and the K107 down to the plate arris, and show the line where they touch.

If they only come close, the wall plate is a straight cold path from the cavity into the roof and the psi-value assumed in SAP is not the detail being built. The cavity barrier is not insulation and does not close that path.

D-208

Verge and gable ladder

Vertical section square to the verge · 1:10 @ A3 landscape

What this sheet is for

It carries the insulation line round the gable and closes the cavity at the verge. The weathering is ordinary; the junction that gets queried is where the cavity insulation stops and the roof insulation starts.

Sequence

Cavity insulation up to the underside of the rafter zone. Cavity barrier over it. Flexible insulation over the inner leaf, tight to the last rafter, so the line is unbroken. Ladder built off the last rafter but one, strapped at 2m centres. Undercloak bedded on the outer leaf, or a proprietary dry verge.

Critical check

The insulation over the inner leaf is the whole detail. The cavity insulation stops at the top of the cavity and the roof insulation starts inboard of the last rafter; without a piece bridging the two, the inner leaf is a cold path the full length of the gable.

The cavity barrier does not do this job. Draw both, label both, and keep them separate.

D-212

Intermediate floor into the external wall

Vertical section between joists · 1:10 @ A3 landscape

What this sheet is for

It resolves the floor zone as an air barrier problem rather than a structural one. The joist bearing is the engineer’s; what building control looks at here is whether the air barrier and the insulation survive the floor.

Sequence

Inner leaf built and parged in one continuous run. Hangers fixed to it, joists dropped in, restraint straps over three joists with solid noggins at 2m centres. Quilt laid between the joists, ceiling fixed, deck glued and screwed.

Critical check

Joists built into the inner leaf punch a hole in the air barrier at every joist and at every strap. Hang them instead and the leaf stays continuous, which is the only version of this junction that survives an air test.

Check the cavity insulation runs past the floor zone without a gap. It is the easiest place on the elevation to leave one, and the hardest to find afterwards.

D-213

Wall to foundation

Vertical section, depth broken · 1:10 @ A3 landscape

What this sheet is for

It is the substructure sheet: what happens between the foundation and the damp proof course. D-201 takes the same wall and resolves the floor edge; this one resolves the foundation, the cavity fill and the change from dense blockwork to the wall above.

Sequence

Trench excavated to the full width and the concrete cast against undisturbed ground. Dense blockwork both leaves. Lean mix cavity fill to 225 below the DPC, topped with a fall to the outside. DPC not less than 150 above finished external ground, weep holes above both the fill and the ground.

Critical check

The depth is broken on this sheet and is not to be scaled. Trench fill is taken to not less than 1000 below finished ground level, and to below the invert of any adjacent drain without undermining it.

The width is the wall plus 300 and never less than 450 — confirm it against Table 10 of Approved Document A or the engineer’s design. Where the wall is thin, wall plus 300 falls below 450 and the 450 governs.

D-218

Door threshold, level access

Vertical section through the opening · 1:10 @ A3 landscape

What this sheet is for

A level threshold removes the 150 step that normally keeps water out, so three things have to do that job instead: the fall on the paving, the channel, and the tray. It is also where the floor insulation and the wall insulation have to meet.

Sequence

Slab and membrane, membrane turned up at the opening. Insulated closer and edge insulation set against it. Cavity tray under the threshold with stop ends, weeping over the channel. Threshold bedded and sealed, paving laid 15 down and falling away.

Critical check

Water and heat are two separate problems at a level threshold and they are solved by different components. The channel and the tray keep water out; the closer and the edge insulation keep the heat in. A sheet showing three of the four gets built with three.

The 15 below finished floor level and the accessible threshold definition are Approved Document M, and the definition is not held in the library — confirm it against Appendix A before this is issued on a job.
